Wash & Darn Wool Socks

Wool socks are tougher than they look. Wash them gently, dry them flat and mend small holes early, and a good pair will keep you warm for many winters.

5 parts6 min readReviewed Nov 2026

1

Wash less often

Because wool resists smell, you can often air socks for a day between wears. When they do need a wash, turn them inside out to protect the outer surface and reduce pilling.

2

Wash gently

  • Use cool or lukewarm water and a wool-friendly detergent.
  • Choose a gentle or wool cycle, or wash by hand.
  • Avoid bleach and fabric softener, which damage fibres.
  • Never use very hot water: heat and rubbing cause felting and shrinking.
3

Dry flat

Gently squeeze out water without wringing. Lay socks flat on a towel or rack to dry, away from radiators and direct sun. Tumble drying on high heat can shrink them by a whole size.

4

Darn small holes early

Thin spots usually appear at the heel and toe. Slip a darning egg, a mushroom or even a light bulb inside the sock. Using matching wool, weave rows of stitches across the hole, then weave across them the other way to make a little patch of new fabric.

5

Store them well

Store clean socks folded, not balled up, which stretches the cuff. In summer, keep them in a drawer with cedar or lavender to discourage moths. Check stored woollens now and then.
