Cookie Policy

How Woolies uses cookies and how you can control them.

Updated November 24, 2026

This Cookie Policy explains how Woolies uses cookies and similar technologies on woolies.com. Please read it together with our Privacy Policy.

What are cookies?

Cookies are small text files stored on your device by websites you visit. They help websites remember preferences and understand how pages are used. Some are set by us and some by third-party services.

Beyond cookies themselves, websites can also use local storage and session storage, which work in a similar way but are read only by the page that set them. Cookies are not programs, they cannot run on your device and they cannot read other files on it. What makes them worth understanding is that some are set by the website you are visiting and some by other companies whose code is on the page, and those two kinds behave very differently when it comes to privacy.

Cookies we use

Essential

We store your cookie-notice choice in your browser’s local storage so the notice does not reappear. This is needed for the website to work as intended.

Live chat

Tawk.to sets cookies and local storage entries such as TawkConnectionTime and twk_uuid_* so a chat can continue if you move between pages or come back later.

Advertising

If ads appear, Google and other vendors may set cookies to show ads based on your previous visits to this and other websites, and to measure ad performance. Manage personalised ads at Google Ads Settings.

We keep this list short on purpose, and we review it whenever we add or remove a service from the website. If a provider changes which cookies it sets, we update this page rather than leaving an out-of-date list in place. None of the entries above are used to build a profile of you across other websites by us, although a third-party advertising provider may do so under its own policy, which is why you can switch non-essential cookies off whenever you like.

Managing cookies

Choose “Accept” or “Essential only” in our cookie notice, or manage cookies in your browser settings. Blocking cookies may affect some features.

  • Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies
  • Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data
  • Safari: Settings → Privacy → Manage Website Data
  • Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions

Browser settings are the most thorough way to control cookies, because they apply to every website rather than just this one. Most browsers also offer a private or incognito window, which clears everything at the end of the session, and an option to block third-party cookies while allowing the ones a site needs to work. Bear in mind that blocking everything can break useful features on other websites, such as staying signed in. On woolies.com, blocking non-essential cookies costs you nothing at all.

Updates

We may update this policy when our tools or legal requirements change. The date above shows the latest version.

We update this page when we add or remove a service, when a provider changes what it sets, or when the law changes what we have to tell you. The date at the top always shows the version you are reading, and we do not make changes quietly. If an update means we need your consent again, the cookie notice will reappear so you can make a fresh choice. You can also reopen that choice at any time by clearing this website’s data in your browser.

If you clear your browser

Clearing cookies and site data removes your saved choice, so the cookie notice will appear again on your next visit. Anything you had typed into the tools on woolies.com is cleared at the same time, because it was only ever held in your browser.

Clearing site data is also the simplest way to change your mind about cookies, because the notice will appear again and you can choose differently. Nothing of yours is lost in the process: there is no account here, no saved work and no history we hold on your behalf. If you use private or incognito windows, everything is cleared automatically when you close them, and the notice will appear on every new visit.

Children

woolies.com is not aimed at children, and we do not knowingly set cookies to build a profile of a child. If you believe a child has used this website and you want their information removed, write to hello@woolies.com.

The guides and tools on woolies.com are written for a general adult audience, and nothing here is aimed at children or designed to appeal to them. We do not ask anybody for their age, and we have no interest in collecting information about young people. If you are a parent or guardian and you believe a child has sent us their details, write to hello@woolies.com and we will remove them promptly and confirm when it is done, without needing a formal request.

How long cookies last

Session cookies are deleted when you close the browser. Persistent cookies stay until they expire or until you clear them. Live chat and advertising cookies typically last from a few days up to two years, depending on the provider that sets them.

You do not have to wait for a cookie to expire. Clearing this website’s data in your browser removes all of them straight away, and most browsers let you do that for a single site without touching the rest. Our own essential storage holds nothing but the choice you made in the notice. Where a third-party provider sets something longer-lived, its own policy explains why, and its link is in the list above.

Other storage we use

Besides cookies, this website uses your browser’s local storage to remember your cookie choice so the notice does not reappear on every page. Anything you type into the tools on woolies.com stays in your browser and is not stored on our side.

Local storage differs from a cookie in one useful way: it is never sent to a server with each request, so it stays on your device and is only read by the page that wrote it. We use it for exactly one thing, which is remembering whether you accepted or declined non-essential cookies. The tools on woolies.com use ordinary page memory, which disappears the moment you navigate away or refresh.

Third-party cookies

Some cookies are set by other companies rather than by us, such as our live chat provider and any advertising partner. We do not control those cookies, and each provider sets them under its own policy.

These are the ones worth paying attention to, because the company that sets them can usually see activity across many websites rather than just this one. We keep the number of third-party services to a minimum for that reason. Choosing “Essential only” in our notice stops the non-essential ones being set from here, and your browser settings can block third-party cookies everywhere if you would rather not think about it site by site.

Do Not Track signals

There is no agreed industry standard for Do Not Track, so browsers handle it differently. Choosing “Essential only” in our cookie notice is the most reliable way to limit non-essential cookies on this website.

The practical problem with Do Not Track is that honouring it was always voluntary, so a signal your browser sends may simply be ignored by whoever receives it. Newer signals such as Global Privacy Control have legal weight in some places, and we treat them as an opt-out where that applies. None of this affects what you can read on woolies.com: every page works the same whether you accept cookies or not.

Refusing cookies

You can refuse non-essential cookies without losing access to anything on woolies.com. Essential storage cannot be switched off from the notice, because the website needs it to remember what you chose.

There is no part of this website hidden behind a cookie wall, no feature that stops working and no reminder nagging you to change your mind. Declining is a perfectly normal choice and the notice will not reappear unless you clear this site’s data. The only storage that remains is the single entry recording what you chose, which is what stops us asking you again on every page.
